PaperEngineArena

Reduction engine

Type a monotone 3-SAT formula. This page runs the reduction's actual generator, gen_on_instance.py, unmodified, in Python compiled to WebAssembly (Pyodide). It returns the exact NetHack level the NP-hardness proof builds for that formula. Then you can play the level. The proof claims: the goal G is reachable from the start @ exactly when the formula is satisfiable.

Formula

Syntax, conditions and size limits
  • Logical form: (x1 ∨ x2) ∧ (¬x1 ∨ ¬x2). Also accepted: v, |, or for ∨; &, and, , for ∧; ~, !, -, not for ¬. A single-literal clause needs no parentheses: x1 ∧ ¬x1.
  • DIMACS-like form: 1 2, -1 -2. Clauses are separated by ,, ; or new lines, literals by spaces.
  • Conditions (the source problem, §4.1 P1–P3): every clause has 1 to 3 distinct variables. All its literals have the same sign (monotone: positive clauses are drawn above the variable line, negative ones below). The variables are x1…xn with n the largest index. Variables that occur in no clause are deleted by the generator (this does not change satisfiability or planarity). The clause/leg system must also have a planar drawing: the generator searches for one and fails if none exists.
  • Limits here: n ≤ 8, m ≤ 8 clauses, and at most 50,000 candidate leg orders per side. The generator is polynomial once the drawing is given (§4.6). Without a drawing it brute-forces the leg order (∏ over variables of kv! candidates per side, kv = number of clauses on that side containing xv). Python in WebAssembly is several times slower than native, and the level for m = 8 already has about 104–105 floor cells and a multi-megabyte Lua script. The limits keep the page responsive. They are not a limit of the reduction.
